**Guardianship matters first:**

# Bad Faith Claims: What You Need to Know

## What Constitutes Bad Faith

Bad faith is a serious allegation. Insurance companies have legal obligations to:
- Investigate claims reasonably and promptly
- Communicate transparently
- Make fair settlement offers based on actual damages
- Not use unfair delay tactics

**Examples that might qualify:**
- Refusing to acknowledge clear liability
- Ignoring medical evidence without explanation
- Repeated requests for the same documentation
- Unexplained delays beyond reasonable timeframes
- Lowball offers with no justification

**"Dragged out" alone may not be bad faith** — it depends on *why* and *how* they're delaying.

## How to Pursue It

1. **Document everything**: Dates of requests, responses, delays, communications
2. **Send a demand letter** outlining:
   - Timeline of the claim
   - Specific unreasonable actions
   - How delays harmed you
   - Request for reasonable settlement + bad faith damages
3. **File a complaint** with your state's insurance commissioner (free, investigates)
4. **Consult a personal injury attorney** who can:
   - Evaluate if bad faith actually applies in your situation
   - Determine what extra damages are recoverable (varies by state)
   - Consider whether to file a separate bad faith lawsuit

## Important Limitations

Bad faith damages aren't guaranteed and require proving intent to harm or reckless disregard — not just incompetence or slowness. Recovery also varies dramatically by state.

**This really warrants an attorney consultation.** Many will review this for free and can tell you if bad faith applies in your jurisdiction.
