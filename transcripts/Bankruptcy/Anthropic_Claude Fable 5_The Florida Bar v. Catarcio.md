# Chapter 7 Eligibility and Your Situation

I can give you general information, but please note this isn't legal advice—bankruptcy has many technical requirements, and consulting a bankruptcy attorney is strongly recommended. Many offer free initial consultations.

## Chapter 7 Eligibility Basics

To qualify for Chapter 7, you generally must:

- **Pass the means test**: If your current monthly income is below your state's median income for your household size, you typically qualify. If it's above, you must complete a more detailed calculation of disposable income.
- **Complete credit counseling**: You must take an approved credit counseling course within 180 days before filing.
- **Not have a recent discharge**: You can't have received a Chapter 7 discharge in the past 8 years (or Chapter 13 in the past 6 years, with exceptions).
- **No recent dismissals**: No bankruptcy case dismissed within the prior 180 days for certain reasons.

## Joint Filing With Your Ex-Wife

**No—you cannot file jointly.** Joint bankruptcy petitions are only available to people who are *currently married* at the time of filing. Since you're divorced, you must each file individually if you both want bankruptcy protection.

Important points about marital debts:

- If you and your ex-wife are **both liable** on a debt, your discharge only protects *you*. Creditors can still pursue her for the full amount (and vice versa).
- **Domestic support obligations** (alimony, child support) are never dischargeable.
- **Property settlement obligations** from your divorce decree are generally *not* dischargeable in Chapter 7 (though they may be in Chapter 13).
- If your divorce decree requires you to pay certain joint debts and you discharge them, your ex-wife may still have claims against you under the decree.

## Listing Your Van and the Sears Debt

- **The van** gets listed on Schedule A/B (property). If you still owe money on it, the lender goes on Schedule D (secured debts), and you'll file a Statement of Intention indicating whether you'll surrender, redeem, or reaffirm the loan. Check your state's motor vehicle exemption—if the van's equity falls within it, you can likely keep it.
- **The Sears debt** typically goes on Schedule E/F (unsecured debts), unless it's a purchase-money secured account tied to specific merchandise.

You must list **all** debts and **all** assets—you can't pick and choose. Omitting anything can jeopardize your discharge.

## Bottom Line

You may well be eligible for Chapter 7 depending on your income, but you and your ex-wife must file separately. Given the divorce-related debt complications, an attorney's guidance would be particularly valuable in your case.

# Exemptions for Your Van and Household Goods

Exemptions are claimed on **Schedule C** of your bankruptcy petition. Which specific exemptions you can use depends heavily on **your state**, so here's how it works:

## Step 1: Determine Which Exemption System Applies

- **Some states let you choose** between the federal bankruptcy exemptions (11 U.S.C. § 522(d)) and your state's exemptions.
- **Many states have "opted out"** of the federal exemptions, meaning you *must* use your state's exemption scheme.
- You generally use the exemptions of the state where you've lived for the **past 730 days (2 years)**. If you moved recently, different rules apply.

You cannot mix and match—you must pick one system entirely.

## Step 2: The Relevant Federal Exemptions (If Available to You)

If your state allows federal exemptions, these are the key ones (amounts adjust every three years, so verify current figures):

| Exemption | Statute | What It Covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Motor vehicle** | § 522(d)(2) | Equity in one vehicle (roughly $4,450 as of the 2022 adjustment) |
| **Household goods** | § 522(d)(3) | Furniture, appliances, clothing, books, etc.—capped per item (~$700) with an aggregate limit (~$14,875) |
| **Wildcard** | § 522(d)(5) | ~$1,475 plus up to ~$13,950 of any *unused* homestead exemption—can be applied to anything, including extra vehicle equity |

## Step 3: Key Points for Your Van

- The exemption protects your **equity**, not the van's full value. Equity = current market value minus what you still owe on any loan.
- If your equity exceeds the vehicle exemption, you may be able to **stack the wildcard exemption** on top (under the federal system, or in some states).
- If the van is still financed, remember the exemption works alongside your **Statement of Intention** (reaffirm, redeem, or surrender).

## Step 4: State Exemptions Vary Widely

State vehicle exemptions range from around $1,000 to $15,000+, and household goods exemptions vary just as much. Some states have generous wildcards; others have none.

## Practical Tips

- Value your van using a realistic **fair market/replacement value** (e.g., private-party value on Kelley Blue Book or NADA), not what you paid for it.
- Value household goods at **garage-sale/used prices**, not replacement cost—most used household items have modest resale value and are easily covered.
- List every item you claim as exempt on Schedule C with the specific statute you're relying on.

**Bottom line:** Tell me your state (or check with a local bankruptcy attorney), because that determines exactly which exemptions you can claim and their dollar amounts. Getting this right matters—an unexempted asset can be sold by the trustee.
