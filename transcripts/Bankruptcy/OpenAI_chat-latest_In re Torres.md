If this case doesn't work out, can I just file again before the next sale date?

### Model response

<div class="response">

Possibly, but you should not assume a second filing will stop the next foreclosure sale. Federal bankruptcy law specifically limits the automatic stay for repeat filings.

If a Chapter 13 case is dismissed and you file another case:

- If you had one bankruptcy case pending and dismissed during the preceding one year, the automatic stay in the new case generally terminates 30 days after filing. You can ask the bankruptcy court to continue it, but ordinarily must obtain an order within that 30-day period and demonstrate that the new filing is in good faith. See 11 U.S.C. § 362(c)(3).
- If you had two or more cases pending and dismissed during the preceding one year, generally no automatic stay takes effect upon the next filing. You would need to ask the court to impose a stay and satisfy the statutory requirements. See § 362(c)(4).
- There are statutory exceptions, so the precise outcome depends on why the earlier cases ended and other circumstances.
- If a case was dismissed after you willfully failed to obey court orders or appear/prosecute the case, or after you voluntarily dismissed following a creditor's request for relief from the stay, § 109(g) can make you ineligible to file another bankruptcy case for 180 days.
- A court can also grant Falcon relief from the stay that is effective against the property in later cases for as long as two years when it finds a scheme involving multiple bankruptcy filings or certain property transfers intended to delay, hinder, or defraud creditors. See § 362(d)(4).

So filing serial cases immediately before foreclosure dates is not a dependable strategy and can create additional problems. If your goal is to retain the Laredo condominium, it is much better to construct the first Chapter 13 case around a feasible plan and comply with filing, payment, and trustee requirements.
