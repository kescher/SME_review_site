## CASE 1: Disciplinary Couns. v. Foreclosure Alternatives, Inc., 127 Ohio St. 3d 455, 940 N.E.2d 971 (2010)

**Opinion**

Opinion PDF

### Case Brief

| Facts | Reasoning | Outcome | Harms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FAI, a for-profit Ohio company, and two nonlawyer agents (Alexakis, Trester) solicited homeowners in foreclosure and contracted, for a fee, to manage their defense and negotiate with lenders; attorneys were hired only to file basic pleadings while the nonlawyers ran strategy. The Chandlers paid $900, were falsely told an attorney had answered, suffered a default judgment, and lost their home. A second homeowner was advised by Trester to stop paying his mortgage and avoid bankruptcy, was told the foreclosure was "stopped," paid from $4,250 in deposits, and also lost his home. None of FAI's people were licensed to practice law. | Nonlawyers practice law when they advise homeowners in foreclosure (including whether to keep paying or file bankruptcy), make representations to creditors on their behalf, and evaluate settlement terms with them. Gov. Bar R. VII(2)(A). FAI and its agents did all three, supplying legal judgment that belonged to an attorney. | UPL found; respondents enjoined. Civil penalties of $15,000 (FAI and Alexakis, jointly and severally) and $5,000 (Trester). | Loss of home to sheriff's sale; fees paid for little real legal service; default entered while clients were misled into thinking the case was handled. |

## CASE 2: Unauthorized Practice of Law Comm. v. Prog, 761 P.2d 1111 (Colo. 1988)

**Opinion**

Opinion PDF

### Case Brief

| Facts | Reasoning | Outcome | Harms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sy Prog, who never attended law school and held no license, agreed to help the Marstons resist two Rule 120 foreclosure proceedings on their real property. He researched the matters, decided the legal strategy, and was responsible for the language, legal issues, and authorities in numerous pleadings, including motions, a demurrer, and a 37-page federal restraining-order action, that the Marstons signed and filed. He also advised them to file related suits against judges and officials, and had them sign a power of attorney to keep them from circulating his pleadings. | One who acts in a representative capacity to protect or defend another's legal rights, and who counsels, advises, and prepares pleadings for them, is practicing law. Prog's research, strategic decisions, advice, and drafting, adopted by clients who merely signed, was the unauthorized practice of law, and the licensing requirement exists to protect the public from exactly this kind of incompetent service. | UPL found. Permanent injunction, a contempt finding, and $7,178.62 in fines and fees plus $1,326.08 in costs; imprisonment declined as it was a first offense. | Concrete: the clients took a $6,678.62 bad-faith attorney-fee judgment, one client was held in criminal contempt and fined, the state paid $4,524.02 in fees the clients could not, and the frivolous filings wasted judicial resources and defamed officials. |
