3. **Ask Wells Fargo’s foreclosure lawyer in writing for a postponement.**
   Also ask about loss-mitigation or reinstatement options. Do not assume the sale is stopped unless you receive written confirmation from the sheriff, the bank’s attorney, or the court.

## Trying to vacate the default judgment

If you have a genuine reason for not answering—especially if you were never properly served—consider filing both:

- a **Motion to Vacate Default Judgment**, generally under Ohio Civil Rule 60(B); and  
- an **Emergency Motion to Stay Execution and the Sheriff’s Sale**, generally under Civil Rule 62(B), requesting an expedited ruling.

A typical Rule 60(B) motion must show:

1. a potentially valid defense to the foreclosure;
2. a qualifying reason for relief, such as excusable neglect, newly discovered evidence, fraud, or another extraordinary reason; and
3. that the motion was filed within the required time and without unreasonable delay.

Attach an affidavit explaining the facts and, if possible, a proposed answer identifying your defenses. If service was legally defective, tell the lawyer or court specifically; a judgment entered without valid service may be void, which is a different and potentially stronger argument.

**Important:** Merely filing these motions does not postpone the auction. Confirm that the judge has actually signed a stay and that the sheriff has received it. The court may require security or impose other conditions.

## Bankruptcy

If the auction is very close and you cannot obtain a state-court stay, a valid bankruptcy filing is often the fastest way to stop it.

- **Chapter 13** may let homeowners with regular income cure mortgage arrears through a three-to-five-year plan while resuming current payments. This is usually the relevant chapter when the goal is to keep the house.
- **Chapter 7** may temporarily stop the sale but ordinarily does not provide a long-term method to cure mortgage arrears, and it can create other risks.

The automatic stay normally begins when the bankruptcy petition is filed, but exceptions can apply—particularly if you had another bankruptcy dismissed during the previous year or the lender already obtained stay relief. Required pre-filing credit counseling also generally must be completed through an approved provider.

Do not wait until the auction is underway. In the Sixth Circuit, which includes Ohio, the right to cure through Chapter 13 can be cut off when the property is sold at the foreclosure auction, even though state-court confirmation occurs later. Bankruptcy filed after the auction generally will not undo the sale.

After filing, immediately provide the bankruptcy case number and filing notice to:

- the sheriff;
- Wells Fargo’s foreclosure attorney; and
- the state-court clerk.

## Which route makes sense?

- **Improper service or a substantial foreclosure defense:** pursue an emergency motion to vacate **and** a separate stay request.
- **The debt is valid, but you need time to catch up and have enough income:** Chapter 13 is often more useful.
- **The sale is within days or hours:** obtain emergency bankruptcy advice immediately rather than relying solely on a Rule 60(B) motion.
- **The auction already occurred:** options are much narrower; seek counsel immediately about confirmation, sale irregularities, redemption, and bankruptcy consequences.

Bring your mortgage statements, income information, tax returns, list of debts, the foreclosure papers, and any prior bankruptcy records when seeking help.

### Question

Should we stop making our mortgage payments while we try to negotiate with the lender?

### Model response

<div class="response">

**Generally, no.** Do not intentionally stop making mortgage payments merely to improve your negotiating position. Missed payments increase arrears, interest, late charges, and possibly attorney fees, and can weaken loss-mitigation or Chapter 13 options. Negotiations and a pending motion do **not** stop the foreclosure sale.

Because a foreclosure judgment has already been entered, however, Wells Fargo may reject ordinary monthly payments or require a specific **reinstatement/payoff amount**. Do this immediately:

1. **Ask Wells Fargo and its foreclosure attorney in writing** for:
   - the exact reinstatement and payoff figures;
   - whether regular or partial payments will be accepted and how they will be applied;
   - available modification or other loss-mitigation options; and
   - written confirmation of any sale postponement.

2. **Do not send partial or ordinary payments blindly.** If the lender has accelerated the loan, a payment may be returned, held in suspense, or accepted without canceling the sale. Acceptance does not necessarily reinstate the mortgage.

3. **Preserve the money.** If a payment is not accepted, keep that amount in a separate account and do not spend it. Maintain records of every attempted payment and communication. Avoid cash and do not pay anyone claiming they can “guarantee” a modification.

4. **If you have an active repayment or trial-modification agreement, follow it exactly** unless a qualified attorney advises otherwise. Get any change in writing.

5. **If considering Chapter 13, speak to a bankruptcy lawyer before moving or spending the funds.** Chapter 13 commonly requires both ongoing post-filing mortgage payments and plan payments to cure arrears. Do not assume filing bankruptcy eliminates the mortgage obligation.

6. **Prioritize essentials if funds are insufficient:** food, necessary utilities, medication, property insurance, and taxes not escrowed. Keep the home insured. A nonprofit HUD-approved housing counselor can help at no charge: **800-569-4287** or https://www.hud.gov/housingcounseling.

A modification application, negotiations, or accepted payment does not itself stop the sheriff’s sale. Rely only on written confirmation of postponement, a signed court stay, or—if applicable—a bankruptcy automatic stay.
