<div class="response">

Because an Ohio dissolution is a **joint proceeding**, Van ordinarily is not “served” as a defendant. The critical requirements are his valid signatures on the joint petition and separation agreement and his participation under oath at the dissolution hearing.

## Recommended procedure

### 1. Send Van the complete, final documents

Send Van through the institution’s legal-mail procedure:

- Joint Petition for Dissolution;
- Complete Separation Agreement;
- Any financial, parenting, or child-support forms he must sign;
- Limited waiver, only if the clerk or judge requires one;
- Motion requesting remote participation; and
- Clear signature and notary instructions.

Van should not sign documents containing blanks or an agreement that may later be changed. If material terms change, both spouses should sign the revised version.

### 2. Have Van sign before the institution’s notary

Van should contact the prison’s legal-services, records, or case-management office and request access to a staff notary. The notarial certificate must identify the state and county **where Van is physically located when he signs**, not automatically Cuyahoga County.

Ask the Cuyahoga clerk whether it requires:

- Original wet-ink signatures;
- Separate signature counterparts;
- Notarization of both the petition and agreement; and
- Any institution verification or affidavit.

Remote online notarization should be used only if the institution permits it and the clerk confirms it will be accepted.

### 3. File Van as a joint petitioner—not as a defendant

The petition should identify both of you as “Joint Petitioners.” Give the court Van’s complete institutional mailing information:

- Institution name and address;
- Van’s inmate number; and
- Any required housing or mail designation.

Ordinarily, no summons is issued because Van has joined in filing the petition. His signature should not be replaced by a power of attorney or by your signing for him.

## If the clerk requests a waiver

Use only a **limited waiver of summons**, not a blanket waiver of appearance or hearing. Suggested language is:

```text

proceeding. I acknowledge that I received and reviewed complete copies of

Institution _____________________
```

Van should sign this before an authorized notary. Do not use language stating that he waives “all appearances,” “all further notice,” or the dissolution hearing.

If the clerk nevertheless directs formal service, follow the clerk’s service instructions rather than trying to serve Van yourself. Service on incarcerated persons is governed by Ohio Civil Rules 4.1 and 4.2 and must be directed to Van at the institution in the manner ordered by the clerk or court.

## 4. Separately request remote participation

A service waiver **does not excuse Van from the hearing**. Ohio law generally requires both spouses to appear and confirm under oath that:

- They signed voluntarily;
- They are satisfied with the separation agreement;
- They want the marriage dissolved; and
- Their disclosures and representations are accurate.

File a joint motion—preferably with the initial petition—requesting that Van participate by secure video conference or, alternatively, telephone. Cite his incarceration and request remote testimony under Ohio Civil Rule 43(A) or other authority the court finds appropriate.

Include:

- Van’s institution and inmate number;
- The institution’s court/video coordinator;
- Telephone and email contact information;
- Available videoconference platform;
- Any advance-notice requirements; and
- A proposed order authorizing the connection.

Also send the filed motion and eventual hearing order to the prison’s legal or litigation coordinator. The judge, not the prison or clerk alone, decides whether remote participation is sufficient.

### Suggested requested relief

```text
Petitioners request that Van [full name] be permitted to appear and testify
under oath by secure video conference or, if video is unavailable, by
telephone. If remote participation cannot be arranged, Petitioners request

```

## 5. Do not assume an affidavit substitutes for the hearing

A notarized agreement, service waiver, deposition, affidavit, or power of attorney ordinarily does not by itself satisfy the dissolution-hearing requirement. If the judge denies remote participation, ask about:

- A continuance;
- An institutional video appearance;
- A transportation order, if feasible; or
- Whether the case must proceed through a different legal procedure.

If Van cannot participate in the manner required by the court, the dissolution may not be granted. A divorce action may then be necessary, even if it remains uncontested.

Finally, verify the current requirements with the **Cuyahoga County Domestic Relations Clerk and the assigned judge’s staff**, because judges may have different procedures for incarcerated litigants and remote testimony. Do this early: Ohio dissolution hearings are ordinarily scheduled within a statutory 30-to-90-day period after filing.

An agreement between you does **not** release either spouse from a creditor’s contract. If a joint debt is assigned to one spouse, include a duty to pay it and indemnify the other. For secured property, address sale or refinancing and what happens if refinancing is impossible.

### Retirement benefits

This is a frequent source of problems. The agreement must clearly say either:

- Each spouse keeps all retirement benefits in that spouse’s name and both waive claims to the other’s benefits; or
- A particular plan will be divided according to a precise formula.

If a plan will be divided, a QDRO, DOPO, or other specialized order may be required. Identify who prepares it, who pays for it, and whether gains, losses, loans, and survivor benefits are included. Do not rely on general language such as “retirement will be divided fairly.”

### Spousal support

Choose clearly between:

- A permanent mutual waiver, with the court not retaining jurisdiction; or
- A specified support award.

If support will be paid, state:

- Amount and frequency;
- Starting and ending dates;
- Payment method;
- Termination events;
- Whether support is modifiable; and
- Whether the court retains jurisdiction over amount or duration.

A permanent waiver ordinarily cannot be undone merely because one spouse later has financial difficulty.

### Taxes and insurance

The agreement should address:

- Filing status for any unresolved tax year;
- Division of refunds and liabilities;
- Cooperation with audits or amended returns;
- Health-insurance responsibility after dissolution;
- Uninsured medical bills incurred before the decree;
- Any continuation coverage; and
- Any required life insurance.

Be cautious about promising that one former spouse can remain on the other’s health plan; divorce or dissolution commonly ends spousal eligibility.

## If you have minor children

The short child-related clause in the template is **not enough by itself**. You normally need detailed provisions and the court’s required parenting and child-support forms covering:

- Whether one parent is residential parent and legal custodian or shared parenting is requested;
- A specific parenting-time schedule;
- Holidays, school breaks, transportation, communication, and exchanges;
- How Van’s incarceration affects current parenting time and telephone/video/contact arrangements;
- Child support calculated under the Ohio guidelines;
- Any deviation from guideline support, with facts supporting the deviation;
- Cash medical support;
- Health-insurance responsibility;
- Division of uninsured medical expenses;
- Child-care and extracurricular expenses;
- Tax dependency claims and any required IRS release;
- School and medical-record access;
- Relocation and notice requirements; and
- Any required parenting proceeding affidavit or shared-parenting plan.

The court decides child-related issues according to Ohio law and the children’s best interests. The parties cannot simply waive child support without an adequate legal basis and court approval. Van’s incarceration may also affect the support calculation, but it should not be assumed that his income is automatically zero.

## Incarceration-specific provisions to add if relevant

Consider adding terms addressing:

- How Van will receive or deliver personal property;
- Who may retrieve property on his behalf;
- Deadlines that account for institutional restrictions;
- Communication through permitted prison channels;
- Responsibility for restitution, fines, commissary debt, or other obligations;
- Whether either spouse has authority over the other’s prison account or benefits;
- Signing deeds, vehicle titles, or retirement orders through a prison notary; and
- What happens if institutional restrictions prevent compliance by a stated deadline.

Do not give either spouse a deadline that is impossible to meet while incarcerated.

## Provisions that should be made more definite

Avoid language such as:

- “We have already divided everything”;
- “Each will pay their own debts” without identifying joint debts;
- “The house will go to her” without addressing the deed and mortgage;
- “Retirement will be handled later”;
- “Reasonable parenting time” without a workable schedule; or
- “Support is reserved” without specifying the scope and duration of retained jurisdiction.

List each significant item, its recipient, related debt, transfer deadline, and enforcement method. Attach schedules if the lists are long.

## Documents required outside the agreement

Even a complete separation agreement does not constitute the entire filing. Depending on your facts and current Cuyahoga County requirements, the packet may also require:

- Joint petition for dissolution;
- Affidavits of income, expenses, property, and debt;
- Confidential personal-identifiers form;
- Health-insurance affidavit;
- Certified marriage record;
- Parenting proceeding affidavit;
- Child-support worksheet and proposed support order;
- Parenting plan or shared-parenting plan;
- Proposed decree of dissolution;

## Bottom line

The template covers the major legal categories, but whether **your completed agreement** covers everything cannot be determined without your actual facts and selected provisions. Before filing, create a complete inventory of both spouses’ assets, debts, income, retirement benefits, pending claims, and—if relevant—children’s issues. Every item should be expressly awarded, divided, or waived, and all optional sections should be resolved rather than left blank. The court can reject the agreement if it is incomplete, ambiguous, or not fair and equitable.
