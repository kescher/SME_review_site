# Service and Waiver for Incarcerated Spouse - Cuyahoga County

## Ohio Legal Requirements

Ohio allows dissolution without Van appearing, but service and waiver must be **strictly compliant**. Here's what you need:

---

## Option 1: Waiver of Service (Preferred for Incarceration)

This is typically the cleanest approach if Van genuinely consents.

**Requirements:**
- Van must sign an **Affidavit and Waiver of Service** acknowledging he received notice and waives formal service
- Must be **notarized** (critical - notarization by a prison notary or outside notary)
- Should include Van's statement that he:
  - Understands the petition and settlement agreement
  - Voluntarily consents to the dissolution
  - Waives his right to formal service
  - Understands he doesn't need to appear in court

**How to execute while incarcerated:**
1. Have a notary visit the facility, OR
2. Use the facility's notary (if available), OR
3. Have Van's attorney (if he has one) facilitate notarization
4. Mail documents to Van; he signs and returns with notarization

---

## Option 2: Personal Service at the Correctional Facility

If Van doesn't sign a waiver, you must serve him formally:

**Process:**
- Sheriff's deputy or process server serves documents at the facility
- The facility must accept service (verify this first—policies vary)
- Incarceration address becomes his service address for the case
- You'll need proof of service filed with the court

**Contact the facility first** to ask:
- Do they accept service of legal documents?
- What's their process?
- Any restrictions on delivery?

---

## Critical Safeguards (Cuyahoga County Courts Require)

The court will scrutinize this. Be prepared to show:

1. **Van's knowing, intelligent waiver** (signed, notarized affidavit)
2. **Evidence he understands the marital settlement terms**
3. **Confirmation he was not coerced** (the court may order a phone call or written statement to him)
4. **Clear chain of custody** for all documents exchanged

1. **Draft your petition and MSA**
