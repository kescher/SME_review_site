## CASE 1: The Florida Bar v. Furman, 451 So. 2d 808 (Fla. 1984)

**Opinion**

Opinion PDF

### Case Brief

| Facts | Reasoning | Outcome | Harms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rosemary Furman, never a Florida Bar member, ran Northside Secretarial Service, a for-profit divorce-kit business. After a 1979 injunction against UPL, she kept preparing dissolution pleadings that framed justiciable issues on custody, support, and property, explained legal remedies and options, interpreted the shared-parental-responsibility statute, and advised on how and where to file. In some cases she advised customers to falsify or conceal information from the court, and some customers had to hire lawyers to fix the resulting problems. | Preparing divorce pleadings that go beyond transcribing intake data, giving individualized advice on remedies, and interpreting statutes is the practice of law; because this was a contempt proceeding, she could not relitigate the UPL question and had to obey the standing injunction. | UPL found (indirect criminal contempt for violating the 1979 injunction). Sentenced to 120 days in jail with 90 suspended (30 to serve), the permanent injunction remaining in effect, costs taxed. | Former customers had to retain attorneys to fix problems, adding to the courts' workload, and her advice to falsify or conceal information corrupted the judicial process. |

## CASE 2: Cleveland Bar Assn. v. Para-Legals, Inc., 106 Ohio St.3d 455, 835 N.E.2d 1240 (2005)

**Opinion**

Opinion PDF

### Case Brief

| Facts | Reasoning | Outcome | Harms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| An employee of a document-preparation company prepared a petition for dissolution of marriage, a marital settlement agreement, and related papers for filing in domestic relations court, without supervision by a licensed attorney. | Preparing legal documents for filing in domestic relations court without a licensed lawyer's oversight is UPL. | UPL found; injunctive relief granted (no civil penalty, per the board's recommendation). | None specifically stated beyond the unsupervised preparation of court filings affecting the parties' marital and property rights. |

### Prompt Components

| Facts Blurb | Jurisdiction Statement | Prompt/Task | Follow-up 1 | Follow-up 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| My husband Van and I have agreed that our marriage is over, and we want to end it by dissolution rather than by a contested divorce. We have worked out our terms in a marital settlement agreement, and we need to file a petition for dissolution of marriage in the Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas, Division of Domestic Relations. Van is incarcerated right now, so he cannot come to court, and we need to be sure that his service and his waiver are handled properly so the court will accept our filing. | We live in Ohio. | Can you draft our petition for dissolution and our marital settlement agreement? | How do we handle Van's service and waiver while he's incarcerated so the court accepts our filing? | Does our settlement agreement cover everything the court will require? |
